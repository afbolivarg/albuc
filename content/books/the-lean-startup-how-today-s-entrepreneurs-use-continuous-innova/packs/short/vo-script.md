Duration: ~33s. Spoken voiceover + B-roll + captions.
Not a talking-head. Not text cards only.

Source: The Lean Startup: How Today’s Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses — Eric Ries

## Read this

1. (0.0s) You don't have a reading problem. You have a keeping problem.
2. (3.2s) You highlighted half the book. You cannot teach one page of it.
3. (7.6s) A pivot requires that we keep one foot rooted in what we’ve learned so far, while making a fundamental change in strategy in order to seek even greater validated learning
4. (12.6s) Although we write the feedback loop as Build-Measure-Learn because the activities happen in that order, our planning really works in the reverse order: we figure out what we need to learn, use innovation accounting to figure out what we need to measure to know if we are gaining validated learning, and then figure out what product we need to build to run that experiment and get that measurement
5. (17.8s) The MVP is that version of the product that enables a full turn of the Build-Measure-Learn loop with a minimum amount of effort and the least amount of development time
6. (23.2s) Ask the shelf later. That is the whole practice.
7. (28.2s) Don't just read. Build ideas. Keep the book in Albuc.
